## 1. One physical law and its exact count identity

Let Y=B0+U, P(B0=1)=p<1/2, E(U|B0)=0, v=p(1-p), s=E U²,
lambda=n s and ell=(v+s)^(-1/2). The actual conditional noises satisfy

`d=a_0+a_1<=d0<min(p,1-p)` and d0<=A. These are physical conditional

them. All resource rows, conditional variances and scores must refer to this
same law.

At x=np+H/ell choose either nearest integer k and put u=x-k, |u|<=1/2,
delta=k-np. Let pi_j be the actual Bin(n,p) masses and W_j the independent
sum of j actual U_1 variables and n-j actual U_0 variables. Then

`F_actual(x)-F_B(k)
 =-sum_{i=0}^k pi_{k-i} P(W_{k-i}>u+i)
   +sum_{i=1}^{n-k} pi_{k+i} P(W_{k+i}<=u-i)`.

Outgoing events are strict, incoming events closed. For
`f(u)=[Phi((delta+u)/sqrt(n(v+s)))-Phi(delta/sqrt(n(v+s)))]/pi_k`,
direct subtraction gives

`rho_B(C-C_B)=sqrt(n)pi_k[Q/pi_k-f(u)]+R`,

where Q is the displayed CDF difference and
`R=sqrt(n)[Phi(delta/sqrt(nv))-Phi(delta/sqrt(n(v+s)))]+C(rho_B-rho)`.
This establishes the signs used by both kinds of witness. Dropping outgoing
terms with i>0 gives an upper bound, not equality. Either representation at a

## 5. Poisson and Bennett: comparison and both exact evaluations

For independent centered X_i<=A and a smoothed positive cube f, f'',f''',
f'''' are nonnegative. The Taylor quotient
`[f(x+y)-f(x)-y f'(x)]/y²=integral_0^1(1-r)f''(x+ry)dr`
increases with y. Therefore
`Ef(x+X)<=f(x)+(Var(X)/A²)*Af(x)`, where
`Af=f(x+A)-f(x)-A f'(x)`.
For the centered-Poisson semigroup P_t,
`d²P_t f/dt²=P_t A²f>=0`, since A²f is the nonnegative double integral
of f'''' with weights(A-r)(A-w). Its tangent at zero dominates the preceding
finite-variable bound. Replacing independent variables successively and
adding an independent centered Poisson variable for any unused variance
proves

`E(S-b)_+³ <= E[A(P_mu-mu)-b]_+³`, mu=V/A².

Translations and expectations preserve the derivative signs. Bounded actual
upper supports and finite Poisson third moments justify removing smoothing.
Markov gives the closed upper-tail bound at any anchor0<=j<b/A+mu;
reflection permits lower tails because actual |X_i|<=A. Thus atoms in the
incoming closed events are included. This is the actual argument in
manuscript8444–8486 and D/structural/low47_new_lemmas.tex:71–119, checked
against the selected LOCAL42 functions.

The LOW helpers use the identity

`E(P_mu-j)_+³=mu³+3(1-j)mu²+(1-3j+3j²)mu-j³
                 +exp(-mu)sum_{k<=floor(j)}mu^k(j-k)³/k!`.

Only the correcting sum is nonnegative; replacing exp(-mu) there by the
reciprocal of the degree24 positive exponential polynomial is safe even when
the raw cubic is negative. Minima over legal anchors and1 remain upper bounds.
The actual mu/anchor/domain gates are retained.

TINY and MID instead sum the positive series through32 and cover the entire
tail from33 by a geometric series. Its successive ratio decreases after33;
the implemented `mu/34*((34-j)/(33-j))³<1` dominates it. The tail's first
term is included. Again the degree24 positive exponential denominator gives
an upper probability. No positive omitted tail is discarded.

Bennett follows from the same monotone Taylor quotient for the exponential,
independence and Chernoff, giving
`B(A,V,b)=exp(b/A)[V/(V+Ab)]^(b/A+V/A²)`.
The selected code reduces the logarithm's argument by powers of two and uses
the positive atanh series for a lower logarithm. This gives a lower positive
exponent, so the reciprocal positive degree16 exponential polynomial at one
sixteenth of it, raised to16, is an upper probability. MID's extra rational
floor of that exponent is conservative. `log_upper` uses its positive
geometric remainder in the opposite direction for the monotonicity test.

On the two scaled TINY phase cells the first positive Poisson index h0
satisfies the exact endpoint gate
`h0-1-mu-3mu/(b/A+mu-j)>0`, with b/A>j. Both subtracted terms increase in
mu, so every positive series term divided by lambda is increasing all the
way from zero to the upper noise endpoint. The comparison uses the exact
Poisson series's monotonicity; its outward finite evaluation is needed only
at the endpoint. No monotonicity of a rounded approximation is assumed.

For later incoming counts the variance/threshold bounds give
`B(A,iV,ib)=B(A,V,b)^i`, hence the complete tail B²/(1-B). Its scaled
monotonicity follows from
`lambda*B'/B=b/A-mu*log(1+b/(A*mu))`: this decreases with mu, and a checked
endpoint lower bound>.5 makes B²/[lambda(1-B)] increase. All later counts,
including harmless added illegal counts, are covered. On phase cells away
from zero, the positive constant own gain instead permits unscaled endpoint
tails, followed by division of the positive bracket by the noise ceiling.
