# Mathematical logic of the resource-to-count chain

Review date: 23 October 2026. This is an independent, bounded argument
review. It is not a fresh numerical certificate acceptance, a complete
verification of every arithmetic kernel, or a proof of the final theorem.

The reviewed resource-to-count chain has no confirmed structural
contradiction in the implications checked below. In particular, the
conditional count laws are derived from the original iid marginal; they
are not an extremal finite-support ansatz. The resource iteration preserves
that same marginal, signed moments and physical defect. There is one small
literal hypothesis missing from the stated normalized-count theorem:
its lower own-count mass must be nonnegative. The representative exact
implementation inspected here satisfies that condition.

It would still be premature to say that all mathematical work is finished.
The broad analytic input and every enabled implementation family have not
received an exhaustive proof-to-code review. The valid conditional
implication below must also be joined to the actual support and resource
acquisitions and a complete fresh count certificate. Historical PASS
objects and source hashes do not establish those mathematical premises.

## Complete Fourier bounds and the sign license

The LOW argument at 1708–1815 expands the actual characteristic function
against the Gaussian/cubic comparator. The following factors were checked
independently from the displayed argument:

* Symmetrization gives the damping exponent `(45-M)/96`; the stated
  `M <= 11.1` gate keeps it positive. The earlier coefficient 43 is
  explicitly rejected in the later discussion. The Taylor cross remainder
  is integrated with the `n-1` or `n-2` damping appropriate to that term.
* The Gaussian integrals yield the displayed `E6/(2 a0^2)` and
  `B6^2/(2 b0^3)` contributions to `L2`. Both the `L0` and `LH` terms
  derive from the same comparator and coefficient choices.
* The paired cancellation norm
  `integral exp(-x^2/2) abs(z x^2-c x^4) dx` is convex in `(z,c)`.
  Bounding it at every coefficient-rectangle corner is valid. Upper
  bounds at those corners need not themselves form a convex function.
* Reflection of this auxiliary absolute Fourier integral changes both
  odd coefficients. It does not reflect the contact law or change the
  signed gamma in the conserved identity (2729–2743, 3404–3463).

The actual-index finite LOW variant at 1965–2052 preserves the integral
on `[0,1/2]`, and its Taylor bounds have a signed remainder. Its formulas
form a coherent triple. Independently minimizing three coordinates from
different triples would need another argument and is not licensed here.

For HIGH, the argument at 2060–2260 is complete in frequency. The
Laguerre rational majorant follows from a nonnegative squared-polynomial
remainder, not a sampled fit. In the window estimate, conditioning on an
actual independent-copy event gives

`abs(f(t))^n <= exp(-n P(E) E[sin^2(td/2) | E])
             <= E[exp(-n a sin^2(td/2)) | E]`.

The second inequality uses `P(E) >= a`, nonnegativity and Jensen. Each
conditional distance may use its own frequency cells because Tonelli is
applied to a nonnegative integrand. The nonzero cores, their wings and
the zero cell are all charged. The Gaussian/cubic comparator remains on
the **entire** `[1/2,infinity)` interval. Cell boundaries have zero
Lebesgue frequency measure even if the conditional distance law has atoms.
Replacing `n` by a floor requires the separately stated monotonicity gates;
it is not monotonicity of the unknown constants `C_n`.

The supported compensated-power alternative at 2289–2353 also retains
the infinite tail. Its telescoping sum uses no division by distinct
roots and does not assume `W <= 1`. The independent triangle bound and
the compensated bound concern the same complete remainder. Valid
whole-cell majorants may be minimized; favorable pieces without their
own complete cell bound may not be selected. The anchored characteristic
function lemma used at 2319 is an earlier analytic input, not proved by
this section or newly audited here.

Weight removal at 2380–2399 is ordered correctly. The first complete
raw bound proves the positivity of the coefficient whose Gaussian weight
is changed. Later passes can then use the unweighted resource. The extra
phase curvature at 2421–2429 is charged once; it cannot subsequently be
added again to an already improved curvature.

## One resource pays for displacement and noise

The positive-measure identity at 2446–2470 is conditional Taylor
expansion about each actual sign-conditional mean. The linear term has
zero conditional expectation and `W^2` is nonnegative. The simplex bound
prices a *sum* of costs against one budget; it does not allow each cost
to consume the full budget before they are added.

The physical gap decomposition at 2481–2533 retains both outer charges,
the binary square and inward movement. Its binary square uses
`sigma^2=10` and `p_*=(4-sigma)/2`. Nonnegativity is established before a
small-defect assertion licenses a genuine central gap. In particular,
zero in the closed support is excluded only by a strict contradiction.

The weighted joint export at 2591–2629 was checked directly. The decisive
chain is

`N d_D >= n D >= n b + 6 n(1-theta) S_* d_Y
                      + K(1-theta) lambda/(pq)
              >= N b + 6 N(1-Theta) S_* d_Y
                      + K(1-Theta) lambda/v_*`.

It needs `n >= N`, `b >= 0`, and a **weighted** premise `nD <= N d_D`.
An unweighted `D <= d_D` does not prove it. Division occurs only for a
positive residual. A zero residual forces both nonnegative coordinates
to vanish, and a negative one excludes the law. Enlarging either
positive intercept weakens the result safely; shrinking an intercept
needs a proved new inequality.

The later signed formulation at 3711–3821 makes the same point. Trading
the displacement term for stronger signed moment prices is valid through
`s_i <= outward_i * inward_i`. That traded term must not be added a
second time to the resulting moment inequality. The original resource
remains separately true and may be intersected with it. Zero outward
amplitudes are deterministic conditional classes and are handled before
division.

The radius feedback at 3928–3940 is also correctly ordered: confinement
first proves a noise radius; only then is that radius converted into a
new support bound and used in higher-moment estimates. The propagation
theorem does not license using a proposed improved support radius in the
argument that is supposed to prove that radius.

## Coefficient rectangles and signed phase

The signed moment bounds at 2750–2835 retain one common first moment.
Their moment-matrix corrections arise from a nonnegative support-localized
measure; a zero correction denominator is handled before division.
The three nonnegative deductions in the constant charge are alternatives:
their maximum may be subtracted, their sum may not.

The central rectangle at 3289–3324 is a complete tensor Bernstein
enclosure. Since the basis is nonnegative and sums to one, keeping both
first-moment endpoints preserves negative as well as positive central
integral coefficients. The resulting maximum of affine functions is
convex in rho. The later four-corner argument takes score/moment
endpoints **before** completing a square, with positive corner divisors.
It is not an assertion that an arbitrary ratio is convex.

The safe signed phase price is

`C rho + (1/2) min(L_phi*g0(rho), U_phi*g0(rho))`.

For a negative lower bound on gamma the upper density endpoint must be
used. The manuscript explicitly makes this choice at 3416–3425 and
3650–3660. Reflection inside LOW cannot supply a nonnegative signed
gamma. The minimum of the affine prices is concave in rho, so its
negative contribution to the upper phase envelope is convex. This
preserves the stated endpoint reduction.

The actual-score charge at 3872–3886 requires the contrary branch
`C >= C_E` and the proved upper enclosure `C_E < .40974`. It splits at
the score kink `.40974`; keeping only the two extreme score corners is
not the displayed proof. The signed phase interface at 3888–3908 checks
each corner difference on the entire consumed phase interval, including
an interior quadratic minimum if present. The joint residual at
3911–3924 changes both resource coordinates together, using the same
physical phase `H=(k+u-np)/sqrt(pq+lambda/n)` as the count comparison.

The finite schedules at 2692–2727 and 3981–4037 therefore give a genuine
induction over valid implications. They do not need a convergence or
extremal-law assumption. Pooling can enlarge a surviving union and weaken
its bounds; already proved exclusions must remain external. Empty active
families do not permit taking an undefined maximum or minimum.

## The count comparison keeps the physical witness

At 4052–4091, `hX=I-p+U` is an identity for the actual marginal, with a
possible zero atom assigned to the nonpositive class. Conditional
independence is justified by first conditioning on the whole sign vector;
all vectors with a given count give the same sum law. Zero noise has
both conditional laws equal to zero, so arbitrary allocation there does
not require division by zero.

The sign in the rho identity is important: it is `-q*mu_0+p*mu_1`,
whereas the gamma identity has `+q*mu_0+p*mu_1` (4157–4168). The physical
defect halfplane at 4264–4291 follows from the same gap defect used by
the resource argument. Substitution gives coefficients
`q[3p(sigma+3)-6a0]` and `p[3q(sigma+1)-6a1]`; inserting the one-sided
cubic bounds gives the displayed `(sigma+7)` and `(sigma+5)` prices.
This is not a separately optimized substitute defect. Lower variance
substitution needs the displayed nonnegative-coefficient gate.

The outgoing polynomial is nonpositive on the closed negative half-line
and vanishes at zero. It therefore minorizes a **strict** outgoing event.
In contrast, Chernoff, exponential excess, Cantelli and stop-loss incoming
bounds are for closed events. The count identity at 4094–4111 retains
exactly this distinction, hence preserves atoms on the event plane.
The stop-loss quadratic majorants and the sharp-MGF Hermite majorant
have nonnegative quadratic coefficients, which justifies replacing a
variance by an upper bound. Their zero-variance cases are explicit.

The common cubic polygon argument is coherent: the incoming upper MGF
is convex in the *same* pair of cubic moments used by the outgoing
affine quartic. Positive matched phase chords preserve convex Bernstein
coefficient functions. Maximization over all vertices, controls and
normalization pieces precedes minimization over the finite strategy menu.
A pointwise minimum of strategies, or clipping a convex incoming bound
pointwise by one, is not covered by this argument (4643–4648).
For a convex numerator and positive affine denominator, the vertex ratio
lemma follows from affine barycentric weights; no sign assumption on the
unclipped numerator is needed. Taking its positive part is legitimate.

The original products at 4717–4724 must be covered before inner
contractions. The nearest-count threshold inequalities at 4710–4716
are an additional premise; omitted threshold tails cannot be discarded.
Both closed children are retained at every strict interior cut. Equality
of a necessary-condition endpoint is not emptiness, and a complete tree
must include all `n+1` count roots.

As a representative source check, the original B0 panel-37 protocol uses
coordinates `(p,lambda,alpha,u,a0)` in `rootbox`/`evaluate`, lines 43–49.
Its `exact35_core.py`, lines 234–260, encloses the actual second inward
endpoint using `a1 <= min(d-a0_lower, physical_sign_cap)` and keeps the
support/variance contractions. The fifth tree coordinate is therefore
the actual `a0`; there is no assumption that the other inward endpoint
saturates the remaining budget. This is one inspected family, not a
proof-to-code certification of all count families.

## Small literal repair to the score theorem

Lines 4655–4658 should explicitly require

`0 <= b_k^- <= b_k(p)` throughout the box.

The proof at 4683 uses
`b_k * P(outgoing) >= b_k^- * P(outgoing) >= b_k^- * L_k`.
Its second step requires `b_k^- >= 0`, especially because a polynomial
lower bound `L_k` may be negative. A negative number can be a valid
lower bound for a probability mass in the ordinary order sense; that
interpretation is insufficient. For example, `b_k^-=-1`, `L_k=-1` and
an outgoing probability zero would falsely assert an outgoing loss at
least one. This is a literal theorem-hypothesis repair, not a confirmed
failure of a selected numerical result.

## What remains outside this conclusion

The following are actual remaining proof-review or proof-composition
obligations, not counterexamples found by this audit:

1. The full attainment/contact and compensated-inversion derivation before
   line 1650, including every endpoint convention and exact conserved
   identity, must be covered by its own review. This note checked the
   resource interface with that identity as an input. The separately
   reviewed Prawitz majorant settles its specific kernel statement; it
   does not settle all of `analytic.common` by association.
2. Every quantitative support/confinement callback must be proved at its
   actual integer and with its actual acquired hypotheses. The compact
   support consequence of contact does not provide a numerical radius
   such as 6 or an inherited radius from another index.
3. The displayed atlas was read throughout, and the derivations singled
   out above were independently checked. This is not an exhaustive
   rederivation of every optional coefficient specialization, every
   rational numerical constant, or every source-family implementation.
   In particular, the directed square-root/exponential/Gaussian primitives,
   all optional moment/phase wrappers, complete HIGH selectors, sharp
   endpoint inversions and stop-loss convolution implementations still
   need complete proof-to-code coverage where enabled.
4. A source pin, deterministic receipt comparison or fresh kernel replay
   does not prove the kernel's mathematical inequality. Conversely, a
   valid inequality does not prove that the runtime used its required
   domains or covered every original root. Both checks are needed.
5. The actual row joins must verify all scalar, affine, signed-phase,
   moment and support bounds in the weakening direction for the same
   law, including closed rho/probability seams. The broad finite
   propagation theorem is conditional on these individual implications;
   it is not an automatic theorem about arbitrary stored contracts.
6. A positive closed-CDF count exclusion covers the opposite discrepancy
   only after applying the argument to the entire reflected marginal and
   acquiring that oriented law's resources. Both signs of `H` in one row
   alone do not do this. The increasing induction at 4776–4813 is valid
   on the equality-inclusive contrary branch, but still requires every
   literal-index support/resource/count exclusion and a proved starting
   predecessor.

The immediate mathematical next step is a finite proof-to-code inventory
of the **enabled** coefficient and count strategies, each attached to its
precise displayed inequality and domain gate, together with the missing
actual acquisition joins. More candidate generation cannot replace that
review. This note does not certify a global or all-index conclusion.
